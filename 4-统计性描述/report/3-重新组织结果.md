## 方法与材料

本研究对中国人群队列中的核基因组线粒体插入序列（NUMTs）进行了统计性描述，重点概括其长度分布、染色体插入位点分布及人群频率结构。输入数据包括基础信息表、严格阈值下的高置信断点信息以及长度信息表。基础信息表用于样本质控，仅纳入质控合格样本。结果显示，基础信息表共包含 8,546 个样本，其中 8,376 个样本通过质控，170 个样本被排除。

质控后，断点信息和长度信息分别按样本进行过滤。断点信息用于构建样本级 NUMT 事件表，长度信息用于构建区域级长度统计表。过滤后共保留 4,017,482 行断点记录和 63,765,079 行长度相关记录，说明主要统计均建立在质控合格样本之上。

NUMT 长度统计基于过滤后的长度信息完成。分析过程中按区域汇总同一区域内的所有支持片段，并记录该区域的支持次数、最短长度、中位长度和最长长度；随后以区域内最大观测长度作为代表值，用于区域级长度汇总。因此，该指标更接近区域支持长度的上界，而非插入片段的直接实验长度。输出结果包括区域级长度表及其摘要表。

插入位点分析基于过滤后的高置信断点信息进行。分析将样本标识与对应的染色体区间联合定义为唯一样本级事件，去除重复断点记录后输出事件表，并进一步统计各染色体的事件数，同时生成适用于核型图绘制的输入文件。该部分反映的是样本级 NUMT 事件在核基因组中的空间分布，而不是跨样本 distinct NUMT 的数量。

distinct NUMT 的人群频率统计基于多样本原始输入完成。分析先按染色体对事件中点进行排序，再以 1,000 bp 为最大间距进行跨样本聚类，生成每个聚类的位置数、覆盖样本数、区间范围及长度摘要。频率定义为携带该聚类的样本数占总分析样本数的比例，总分析样本数为 8,372。依据预设规则，聚类被划分为 common、low-frequency、rare 和 ultra-rare，并附加 singleton、doubleton 和 tripleton 标记。主结果采用最低支持样本数为 1 的设定，同时输出最低支持样本数为 2 至 10 的敏感性分析结果。

整个流程由脚本化分析流程调度，完成样本过滤、长度表构建、事件表构建和跨样本频率聚类，并生成核型图和聚类分布图。分析采用 8 线程和每次 1,000,000 行的分块策略。由于当前结果目录未提供软件及依赖包的具体版本，本报告不补造版本信息；同时，当前输出仅支持描述性统计解释，不支持分组推断或因果外推。

## 结果

### R01. 质控过滤结果

基础信息表共纳入 8,546 个样本，其中 8,376 个通过质控，整体通过率为 98.01%。断点信息中共观察到 9,798 个样本，过滤后保留 8,372 个实际出现在断点数据中的合格样本，删除 1,426 个样本；断点记录数由 4,755,276 行降至 4,017,482 行，保留比例为 84.48%。长度信息中共观察到 6,872 个样本，过滤后保留 5,706 个合格样本，删除 1,166 个样本；记录数由 76,418,787 行降至 63,765,079 行，保留比例为 83.44%。

这些结果说明，后续分析建立在较高样本保留率的基础上，整体样本框架较稳定。不过，断点信息与长度信息所覆盖的观测样本数并不完全一致，提示不同上游结果模块的样本覆盖范围存在差异，因此各分析部分的分母不能直接互换。由于当前输出未提供被剔除样本的人群结构信息，尚无法判断质控步骤是否引入系统性偏倚。

### R02. NUMT 长度分布

区域级长度统计共识别 111,487 个 NUMT 区域。以每个区域内的最大支持片段长度作为代表值后，最短长度为 29 bp，最长长度为 16,568 bp，均值为 350.38 bp，中位数为 111 bp。分位数结果显示该分布明显右偏：25%、50% 和 75% 分位数分别为 79 bp、111 bp 和 127 bp，而 90%、95% 和 99% 分位数分别上升至 497 bp、611 bp 和约 8,012.56 bp。进一步统计显示，92,373 个区域（82.86%）长度小于 200 bp，仅 3,407 个区域（3.06%）达到或超过 1,000 bp，2,216 个区域（1.99%）达到或超过 5,000 bp。

上述结果表明，该队列中的 NUMT 以短片段为主，但存在少数显著拉长整体分布尾部的长区段。均值明显高于中位数，提示总体均值受超长区域影响较大。区域内 hit 数的中位数为 14，而 90% 分位数已达 671，也支持部分区域存在极高的支持信号密度。需要强调的是，本分析中的长度代表值为区域内最大 hit 长度，因此更适用于描述“观测支持上界”，而不是严格意义上的插入真实长度。

### R03. NUMT 插入位点的染色体分布

对断点记录去重后，共得到 123,863 个唯一样本级 NUMT 事件。事件广泛分布于常染色体和性染色体，但分布明显不均匀。事件数最多的染色体依次为 chr1（17,519，14.14%）、chr2（17,039，13.76%）、chr11（10,869，8.78%）、chr17（9,716，7.84%）、chr5（8,211，6.63%）和 chr4（8,040，6.49%）；相比之下，chr19、chr18 和 chr22 上的事件较少。

若结合区域级长度表观察各染色体的区域承载数，chr2、chr1、chr11、chr17 和 chr4 仍位居前列，与事件计数结果总体一致。这种一致性提示，高负荷染色体并非仅由少数高重复样本驱动，而更可能反映这些染色体上存在更频繁或更易被检测到的 NUMT 区域。核型图与热图输出为该非均匀格局提供了可视化支持。然而，由于当前结果未按染色体长度、重复序列背景或可比对性进行标准化，本研究只能确认“观测分布不均”，尚不能将其直接解释为真实生物学插入偏好。

### R04. distinct NUMT 的总体频率结构

在最低支持样本数为 1 的主分析中，共识别 2,835 个 distinct NUMT 聚类。支持样本数分布高度偏斜，其 25%、50% 和 75% 分位数分别为 1、2 和 6，表明至少一半的 distinct NUMT 仅出现在不超过 2 个样本中；而 90%、95% 和 99% 分位数分别达到 63、约 308.7 和约 3,071.84 个样本，提示上尾明显延展。换算为样本频率后，中位频率约为 0.020%，75% 分位频率约为 0.061%，而 99% 分位频率可达 31.27%，说明群体中同时存在大量极低频事件和少数高度共享事件。

按预设频率类别划分，ultra-rare cluster 有 2,244 个，占全部 distinct NUMTs 的 79.15%；rare cluster 为 354 个，占 12.49%；low-frequency cluster 为 119 个，占 4.20%；common cluster 为 118 个，占 4.16%。因此，该队列中的 distinct NUMT 频谱以超低频层级为主，真正达到 common 水平的 cluster 数量相对有限。由于 singleton、doubleton 和 tripleton 均包含在 ultra-rare 类别中，这一超低频层既包含个体特异事件，也包括少量低度复发事件。现有数据足以支持对频谱结构的描述，但不足以区分这些 ultra-rare 事件究竟源于真实新近插入、检测边界还是人群历史累积。

### R05. 频率阈值敏感性与低频成分贡献

支持样本阈值分析进一步表明，低频成分主导了 distinct NUMT 的总量变化。当最低支持阈值由 1 提高到 2 时，distinct NUMT 数量从 2,835 降至 1,450，减少 1,385 个，恰与全部 singleton clusters 数量一致；阈值提高到 3 时，总数降至 1,079，再减少 371 个，对应全部 doubleton clusters；阈值提高到 4 时，总数降至 909，又减少 170 个，对应全部 tripleton clusters。此后阈值从 4 增至 10 时，distinct NUMT 数量仅由 909 逐步降至 591，下降速度明显放缓。

这一模式说明，主分析中 distinct NUMT 总数对最小支持阈值非常敏感，而这种敏感性几乎完全来自 1 至 3 个样本支持的超低频部分。一旦剔除 singleton、doubleton 和 tripleton，剩余 cluster 集合趋于稳定。例如，至少在 10 个样本中出现的 cluster 仍有 591 个，而至少在 20、50 和 100 个样本中出现的 cluster 仍分别达到 443、304 和 237 个。这提示该队列同时包含大量个体化或极低频 NUMT，以及一组稳定复发的共享 NUMT。当前分析的局限是仅考察了支持样本阈值变化，尚未评估聚类间距或分母定义变化所带来的影响。

### R06. 高复发 distinct NUMT 热点

最高复发的热点位于 chr17，共有 9,822 个样本支持，频率达到 99.99%，几乎覆盖全部纳入频率分析的样本。其后依次为 chr1 上的热点（7,947 个样本，80.90%）、chr11 上的热点（6,776 个样本，68.98%）、chr5 上的热点（6,120 个样本，62.30%）和 chr2 上的热点（5,984 个样本，60.92%）。这些热点均属于 common 类别，所对应的区间长度范围为 1,731 至 7,572 bp。

这些极高复发 cluster 很可能构成该队列中最稳定的 NUMT 热点区域，并在总体频率谱的 common 端起到决定性作用。不过，这种高度共享既可能反映真实高频背景，也可能受到参考序列结构、比对复杂性或上游 calling 规则的影响。由于当前结果缺少外部数据库比对和独立实验验证，现阶段只能确认这些位点在本队列中高度复发，而无法进一步界定其形成机制。

## 讨论

总体而言，中国人群队列中的 NUMT 图谱呈现出三个彼此关联的特征：长度分布以短片段为主但具有明显长尾，插入位点在染色体间分布不均，distinct NUMT 的频率谱以 ultra-rare 事件占主导但同时保留少量高复发 common 热点。这一组合特征说明，NUMT 在群体中的分布并非简单随机，而更像是由大量低频局部事件叠加少数稳定共享位点共同构成的复合图景。

长度结果提示，大多数可检测 NUMT 可能仅保留较短的线粒体来源片段，或当前流程对短而边界清晰的插入更敏感；与此同时，少数超长区域提示部分位点可能承载更复杂的局部结构。空间分布结果则显示 chr1、chr2、chr11 和 chr17 等染色体在事件数和区域数两个层面均表现突出，支持 NUMT 存在热点化趋势，但当前证据尚不足以区分这种格局究竟来自真实生物学偏好，还是部分源于染色体长度、重复序列环境和可比对性差异。

频率层面的结果进一步表明，该队列中的 NUMT 以超低频事件为主体。阈值敏感性分析显示，singleton、doubleton 和 tripleton 对总量贡献极大；但在更高阈值下，仍保留数百个稳定 cluster，提示群体中同时存在大量个体特异事件和一组跨样本复发的核心位点。尤其是 chr17、chr1、chr11、chr5 和 chr2 上的 common 热点，具有潜在的重要生物学和技术意义，在后续开展人群比较、疾病关联或黑名单过滤前，均应优先进行数据库交叉注释、局部复杂性评估和独立验证。

本研究的局限主要在于其描述性属性。当前结果目录未提供分组统计、结局关联、外部验证或完整的软件版本信息，因此不能将这些结果外推为疾病机制或进化结论。此外，distinct NUMT 的定义依赖 1 kb 聚类规则，长度统计依赖区域内最大 hit 长度，两者都可能影响低频 cluster 或长片段区域的精确刻画。尽管如此，本研究仍为后续工作提供了可靠的经验基础，即中国人群 NUMT 景观具有短片段主导、染色体分布不均和频率长尾显著三大特征。

## Methods and Materials

This study provides a statistical description of nuclear mitochondrial DNA segments (NUMTs) in a Chinese population cohort, focusing on their length distribution, chromosomal insertion-site distribution, and population frequency structure. Input data included a metadata table, high-confidence breakpoint information generated under strict thresholds, and a length-related summary table. The metadata table was used for sample-level quality control, and only QC-qualified samples were retained. The results showed that the metadata table contained 8,546 samples, of which 8,376 passed QC and 170 were excluded.

After QC filtering, breakpoint information and length-related information were processed separately at the sample level. Breakpoint information was used to construct sample-level NUMT event tables, whereas length-related information was used to construct region-level length summaries. After filtering, 4,017,482 breakpoint rows and 63,765,079 length-related rows were retained, indicating that the main summaries were based on QC-qualified samples.

NUMT length statistics were derived from the filtered length-related information. The workflow grouped all supporting fragments belonging to the same region and recorded the number of supports together with the minimum, median, and maximum lengths for that region. The maximum observed length within each region was then used as the representative value for region-level summaries. Accordingly, this metric should be interpreted as an upper bound of supported regional length rather than a direct experimental estimate of true insertion length. The outputs include both a region-level length table and a summary table.

Insertion-site analysis was performed on the filtered high-confidence breakpoint information. The workflow defined a unique sample-level event by combining the sample identity with the corresponding chromosomal interval. After removing redundant breakpoint rows, the analysis generated an event table, summarized event counts by chromosome, and created an input suitable for ideogram plotting. This component therefore reflects the genomic distribution of sample-level NUMT events rather than the number of cross-sample distinct NUMTs.

Population-frequency analysis of distinct NUMTs was performed using multi-sample raw input. Events were first sorted by chromosome and midpoint, and then clustered across samples using a maximum gap of 1,000 bp. For each cluster, the workflow recorded the number of positions, number of supporting samples, genomic span, and length summary. Frequency was defined as the proportion of samples carrying a given cluster among the 8,372 samples included in the frequency analysis. Clusters were classified as common, low-frequency, rare, or ultra-rare according to the predefined scheme, with singleton, doubleton, and tripleton labels added when applicable. The primary analysis used a minimum support threshold of one sample, and sensitivity outputs for thresholds of two to ten samples were generated in parallel.

The overall workflow was implemented through a scripted analysis pipeline that performed sample filtering, length-table construction, event-table construction, cross-sample frequency clustering, and the generation of ideogram and cluster-distribution plots. The analysis used eight threads and a chunk size of 1,000,000 rows. Because the current result directory does not provide exact software or dependency versions, this report does not invent software-version details. In addition, the current outputs support descriptive interpretation only and do not support group-level inference or causal extrapolation.

## Results

### R01. Quality-control filtering

The metadata table contained 8,546 samples, of which 8,376 passed QC, corresponding to an overall pass rate of 98.01%. In the breakpoint information, 9,798 samples were observed, and 8,372 QC-qualified samples remained after filtering, meaning that 1,426 samples were removed. The number of breakpoint rows decreased from 4,755,276 to 4,017,482, yielding a retention rate of 84.48%. In the length-related information, 6,872 samples were observed, and 5,706 QC-qualified samples remained after filtering, meaning that 1,166 samples were removed. The number of records decreased from 76,418,787 to 63,765,079, corresponding to a retention rate of 83.44%.

These results show that the downstream analyses were conducted on a dataset with a high overall retention rate, indicating a relatively stable sample framework. However, the breakpoint information and length-related information did not cover exactly the same observed sample set, implying differences in upstream data coverage. The denominators of the different analysis modules therefore cannot be used interchangeably. Because the current outputs do not provide the population structure of excluded samples, it remains unclear whether QC filtering introduced systematic bias.

### R02. NUMT length distribution

Region-level length analysis identified 111,487 NUMT regions. When the maximum supported fragment length within each region was used as the representative value, the minimum length was 29 bp, the maximum length was 16,568 bp, the mean was 350.38 bp, and the median was 111 bp. The percentile profile showed a strongly right-skewed distribution: the 25th, 50th, and 75th percentiles were 79 bp, 111 bp, and 127 bp, respectively, whereas the 90th, 95th, and 99th percentiles rose to 497 bp, 611 bp, and approximately 8,012.56 bp. Additional counting showed that 92,373 regions (82.86%) were shorter than 200 bp, whereas only 3,407 regions (3.06%) were at least 1,000 bp and 2,216 regions (1.99%) were at least 5,000 bp.

These results indicate that NUMTs in this cohort are dominated by short fragments, but a small number of extended regions create a pronounced long tail. The mean is clearly higher than the median, indicating that a limited number of very long regions strongly influence the overall average. The median hit count per region was 14, whereas the 90th percentile already reached 671, further supporting the presence of extremely dense signal accumulation in some regions. Importantly, the representative length in this analysis is the maximum hit length within a region, so it is more appropriate for describing the upper bound of observable support than the exact biological insertion length.

### R03. Chromosomal distribution of NUMT insertion sites

After deduplication of breakpoint records, 123,863 unique sample-level NUMT events were identified. These events were widely distributed across autosomes and sex chromosomes, but the distribution was clearly uneven. The chromosomes with the highest event counts were chr1 (17,519; 14.14%), chr2 (17,039; 13.76%), chr11 (10,869; 8.78%), chr17 (9,716; 7.84%), chr5 (8,211; 6.63%), and chr4 (8,040; 6.49%), whereas chr19, chr18, and chr22 carried comparatively few events.

When the region-level length table was used to examine the number of regions carried by each chromosome, chr2, chr1, chr11, chr17, and chr4 remained among the leading chromosomes, broadly consistent with the event-count pattern. This agreement suggests that the chromosomes with high event burdens were not driven solely by a small number of highly repeated samples, but more likely reflect chromosomes harboring more frequently observed or more readily detectable NUMT regions. The ideogram and heatmap outputs visually support this non-uniform pattern. However, because the current results were not normalized for chromosome length, repeat background, or mappability, the present study can only conclude that the observed distribution is uneven and cannot directly attribute it to true biological insertion preference.

### R04. Global frequency structure of distinct NUMTs

In the primary analysis using a minimum support threshold of one sample, a total of 2,835 distinct NUMT clusters were identified. The supporting-sample distribution was highly skewed, with the 25th, 50th, and 75th percentiles at 1, 2, and 6 samples, respectively, indicating that at least half of all distinct NUMTs occurred in no more than two samples. By contrast, the 90th, 95th, and 99th percentiles reached 63, about 308.7, and about 3,071.84 samples, respectively, indicating a pronounced upper tail. After conversion to sample frequency, the median frequency was about 0.020%, the 75th percentile frequency was about 0.061%, and the 99th percentile frequency reached 31.27%, showing coexistence of many extremely low-frequency events and a small number of highly shared events.

According to the predefined frequency classes, 2,244 clusters were ultra-rare, accounting for 79.15% of all distinct NUMTs; 354 were rare (12.49%); 119 were low-frequency (4.20%); and 118 were common (4.16%). Thus, the frequency spectrum of distinct NUMTs in this cohort is dominated by the ultra-low-frequency layer, whereas relatively few clusters reach the common range. Because singleton, doubleton, and tripleton clusters are all included within the ultra-rare category, this low-frequency layer contains both individual-specific events and mildly recurrent events. The current data are sufficient to describe the frequency spectrum, but they are not sufficient to distinguish whether these ultra-rare events reflect true recent insertions, detection boundaries, or accumulated population history.

### R05. Threshold sensitivity and contribution of low-frequency components

Support-threshold analysis further showed that low-frequency components dominate the total number of distinct NUMTs. When the minimum support threshold increased from 1 to 2, the number of distinct NUMTs decreased from 2,835 to 1,450, a reduction of 1,385, exactly matching the number of singleton clusters. Increasing the threshold to 3 reduced the total to 1,079, eliminating another 371 clusters and corresponding to all doubleton clusters. Increasing the threshold to 4 reduced the total further to 909, eliminating another 170 clusters and corresponding to all tripleton clusters. Thereafter, when the threshold increased from 4 to 10, the total number of distinct NUMTs decreased only gradually from 909 to 591.

This pattern shows that the total distinct NUMT count in the primary analysis is highly sensitive to the minimum support threshold, and that this sensitivity is driven almost entirely by the ultra-low-frequency component supported by one to three samples. Once singleton, doubleton, and tripleton clusters are removed, the remaining cluster set becomes comparatively stable. For example, 591 clusters were still present in at least 10 samples, whereas 443, 304, and 237 clusters remained at thresholds of 20, 50, and 100 samples, respectively. This suggests that the cohort contains both a large number of individualized or extremely rare NUMTs and a stable set of recurrent shared loci. A limitation is that the present sensitivity analysis evaluated only changes in support thresholds and did not assess alternative clustering gaps or denominator definitions.

### R06. Highly recurrent distinct NUMT hotspots

The most recurrent hotspot was located on chr17, supported by 9,822 samples and reaching a frequency of 99.99%, thus covering nearly all samples included in the frequency analysis. It was followed by hotspots on chr1 (7,947 samples; 80.90%), chr11 (6,776 samples; 68.98%), chr5 (6,120 samples; 62.30%), and chr2 (5,984 samples; 60.92%). All of these hotspots belonged to the common class, and their corresponding region lengths ranged from 1,731 to 7,572 bp.

These highly recurrent clusters likely define the most stable NUMT hotspot regions in this cohort and strongly shape the common end of the overall frequency spectrum. However, such extensive sharing may reflect not only true high-frequency background events, but also reference-sequence structure, alignment complexity, or characteristics of the upstream calling rules. Because the current outputs do not include external database comparisons or independent experimental validation, the present interpretation can only conclude that these loci are highly recurrent within this cohort and cannot yet determine their origin.

## Discussion

Overall, the NUMT landscape in this Chinese cohort shows three closely connected features: a length distribution dominated by short fragments but accompanied by a pronounced long tail, an uneven chromosomal distribution of insertion sites, and a frequency spectrum dominated by ultra-rare events but retaining a small number of highly recurrent common hotspots. Together, these findings indicate that NUMTs in the cohort are not distributed in a simple random manner, but instead form a composite pattern shaped by numerous low-frequency localized events plus a limited number of stable shared loci.

The length results suggest that most detectable NUMTs may retain only short mitochondrial-derived fragments, or that the current workflow is more sensitive to insertions that are short and have clearer boundaries. At the same time, the presence of a few very long regions suggests that some loci may harbor more complex local structures. The spatial distribution results further show that chr1, chr2, chr11, and chr17 rank highly in both event count and region count, supporting the presence of hotspot-like clustering. However, the current evidence is insufficient to determine whether this pattern reflects true biological preference or is partly shaped by chromosome length, repeat environment, and mappability.

The frequency spectrum further indicates that NUMTs in this cohort are dominated by ultra-low-frequency events. Threshold sensitivity analysis shows that singleton, doubleton, and tripleton clusters contribute substantially to the total count; however, several hundred clusters remain even under more stringent thresholds, indicating coexistence of many individualized events and a core group of recurrent shared loci. In particular, the common hotspots on chr17, chr1, chr11, chr5, and chr2 may have important biological and technical relevance, and they should be prioritized for database-based annotation, local-complexity assessment, and independent validation before being used in population comparison, disease association, or blacklist filtering.

The main limitation of this study lies in its descriptive nature. The current result directory does not provide group-level statistics, outcome associations, external validation, or complete software-version information, so these findings should not be extrapolated into disease mechanisms or evolutionary conclusions. In addition, the definition of distinct NUMTs depends on a 1 kb clustering rule, and the length summary depends on the maximum hit length within a region; both factors may influence the precise characterization of low-frequency clusters and long-fragment regions. Even so, the present analysis provides a reliable empirical foundation by showing that the NUMT landscape in this Chinese cohort is characterized by short-fragment predominance, uneven chromosomal distribution, and a strongly long-tailed frequency spectrum.
